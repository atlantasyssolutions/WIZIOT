## The Geography of Fuel Theft on the Central Corridor

Understanding where and how fuel disappears along East African transit routes is essential for implementing targeted telematics defenses:

1. **Long-Haul Rest Stop Siphoning:** Drivers parking overnight at high-density rest stops (e.g., Chalinze, Kahama, and Nyakanazi in Tanzania) siphon 40 to 100 liters per night into 20-liter jerrycans sold to roadside fuel vendors.
2. **Gradient Consumption Disguises:** Drivers exploit steep elevation gains (such as the Kitonga hills or Rwandan mountain passes) to attribute massive fuel usage to "heavy engine load," masking siphoning events.
3. **Border Queue Drainage:** Multi-day customs delays at Rusumo (Tanzania/Rwanda) and Mutukula (Tanzania/Uganda) border posts provide extended unmonitored windows for driver fuel drains.

## 4 Technical Controls to Stop Central Corridor Fuel Theft

### 1. High-Precision Capacitive In-Tank Fuel Probes (±0.2% Accuracy)

Standard mechanical float sensors fail to detect partial siphoning due to wide measurement dead-bands. WizIOT capacitive probes submerge directly into fuel tanks, continuously measuring fuel height with sub-liter resolution.

- **Dual-Tank Summation:** Heavy haulage trucks transiting the Central Corridor carry dual tanks (500L + 500L). WizIOT multi-tank algorithms mathematically sum volume across both tanks simultaneously, preventing false alarms caused by fuel transferring between tanks on mountain slopes.
- **Thermal Density Compensation:** Temperature sensors inside capacitive probes compensate for diesel volume expansion caused by coastal heat in Dar es Salaam (34°C) versus cold highveld nights in Kigali (14°C).

### 2. Ignition-Off Siphoning Detection & Real-Time Alerts

Over 85% of siphoning occurs while the truck engine is turned off overnight. WizIOT telematics units remain active in low-power sampling mode while parked.

If fuel volume drops by more than 3.5 liters without ignition activity, the unit transmits an immediate **Unauthorized Fuel Drain Alarm** via SMS and WhatsApp to control rooms in Dar es Salaam or Kampala within 15 seconds.

### 3. CAN-Bus ECU Fuel Consumption vs. Distance Audit

To stop drivers from blaming steep mountain climbs for missing fuel, WizIOT taps directly into the truck ECU (J1939 CAN-bus protocol).

The system tracks exact engine fuel burn per kilometer, comparing actual engine consumption against physical tank level drops. If a driver claims 400 liters burned on a flat stretch where engine ECU logs show only 280 liters consumed, an automated **Fuel Consumption Variance Exception** is flagged instantly.

### 4. Quad-SIM Cross-Border Telematics Redundancy

Cross-border transit between Tanzania, Uganda, and Rwanda exposes single-SIM trackers to coverage blackouts. WizIOT telematics units house 4 active SIM slots (Vodacom Tanzania, MTN Uganda, Airtel Rwanda, and eUICC Global SIM), maintaining continuous connection and instant alarm transmission across border posts.

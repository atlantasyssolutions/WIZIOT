## The Operational Realities of Mining Fuel Theft in West Africa

Fuel theft in mining transport concessions differs significantly from highway commercial fleet theft:

1. **High-Volume Tank Siphoning:** Heavy mining dump trucks and haulage road-trains carry massive fuel tanks. Thieves siphon 100 to 250 liters in a single event into commercial drums hidden in surrounding bush land.
2. **Fuel Bowser & Dispenser Skimming:** Mobile fuel bowsers dispensing diesel to excavators and haul trucks are skimmed during field refueling, with unrecorded fuel sold to illegal mining (galamsey) operators.
3. **Off-Hours Auxiliary Engine Idling Claims:** Drivers claim high diesel consumption was caused by "24-hour auxiliary engine idling" during heavy rain or site loading delays.

## 4 Engineering Controls for Mining Telematics & Fuel Security

### 1. Ultra-Ruggedized Capacitive Fuel Probes (IP68 / Polyurethane Potted)

### 3. CAN-Bus Engine Load & Torque Fuel Audit

WizIOT connects directly to machine ECUs (J1939 protocol for Caterpillar, Komatsu, Volvo, and Scania mining haulers), capturing:
- True Engine Hours & Load Factor Percentage
- Actual ECU Fuel Burn Rate (Liters per Hour)
- Hydraulic PTO Engagement Timestamps

The software automatically calculates expected fuel usage per shift:

$$\text{Shift Expected Fuel} = \text{Engine Hours} \times \text{Average Liters/Hour} \times \text{Load Factor}$$

If physical fuel tank drop exceeds calculated engine fuel burn by more than 5%, WizIOT flags an immediate **Mining Fuel Variance Discrepancy**.

### 4. Smart Geofenced Concession Boundaries & Fuel Bowser Integration

- **Unauthorized Route Deviations:** If a haul truck strays outside approved mining concession boundaries, WizIOT triggers immediate **Concession Breach Alarms**.

# ATEX Zone 0 & Zone 1 Certified Fuel Sensors for Petroleum Tankers

Transporting bulk petroleum, jet fuel, diesel, and liquid chemicals across the Middle East, GCC, and North Africa involves navigating extreme environmental heat and volatile explosive atmospheres. Installing standard, non-certified electronic tracking hardware inside or around fuel compartments creates catastrophic electrical spark hazards.

To protect human life, fleet assets, and environmental safety, oil companies and transport contractors must enforce strict adherence to ATEX (Appareils destinés à être utilisés en Atmosphères Explosibles) Directive 2014/34/EU and IECEx standards.

---

## What is ATEX Zone 0 and Zone 1 Certification?

ATEX regulations categorize explosive gas and vapor environments into hazardous zones based on the frequency and duration of explosive atmospheres.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        ATEX HAZARDOUS ZONE CLASSIFICATIONS                        |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  ZONE 0: Continuous explosive vapor inside fuel tank (Requires ATEX Zone 0 Probe) |
|  ZONE 1: Occasional explosive vapor near discharge valves (Requires ATEX Zone 1)  |
|  ZONE 2: Unlikely explosive vapor around outer truck cabin (Standard IP67 Enclosure)|
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Hazardous Area Definitions:
* **ATEX Zone 0:** An atmosphere where explosive gas/fuel vapor mixtures are present continuously or for long periods (e.g., inside the fuel tank ullage space).
* **ATEX Zone 1:** An atmosphere where explosive gas mixtures are likely to occur in normal operation (e.g., around discharge valves, vapor recovery lines, and loading manifolds).
* **Intrinsic Safety (Ex ia / Ex ib):** An engineering design protection technique where electrical energy within sensor circuitry is strictly limited below the threshold required to ignite explosive fuel vapors, even under short-circuit fault conditions.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Q1: Why can’t I use standard commercial fuel sensors on petroleum tankers?
Standard fuel sensors are not intrinsically safe. In the event of an electrical short or static discharge, non-ATEX sensors can generate micro-sparks capable of igniting fuel vapor inside the tank, causing catastrophic explosions.

### Q2: What is the difference between ATEX Zone 0 and ATEX Zone 1?
Zone 0 applies to areas where explosive vapors are present continuously (such as inside the fuel tank). Zone 1 applies to areas where explosive vapors occur occasionally during normal fueling or discharge operations.

### Q3: How does temperature compensation work on WizIOT ATEX fuel probes?
Fuel volume expands and contracts with ambient temperature changes. WizIOT ATEX probes feature internal RTD temperature sensors that automatically normalize fuel volume readings to +15°C standard reference temperatures.

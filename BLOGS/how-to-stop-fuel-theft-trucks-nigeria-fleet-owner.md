## The 12-Step Fuel Loss Prevention Framework

### Step 1: Install High-Precision Capacitive In-Tank Fuel Probes
Replace unreliable factory arm float sensors with immersion capacitive probes featuring ±0.2% measurement precision. Capacitive probes measure actual fuel height continuously, providing sub-liter volume resolution across 500-liter to 1,000-liter dual aluminum tanks.

### Step 2: Implement Temperature-Compensated Density Analytics
Fuel expands in afternoon heat and contracts at night. WizIOT sensors feature internal temperature probes that automatically adjust volume readings for thermal expansion, eliminating false siphoning alarms caused by ambient temperature swings in Northern Nigeria.

### Step 3: Enforce Ignition-Off Siphoning Alarms
Over 80% of diesel siphoning occurs while trucks are parked overnight at unmonitored rest stops in Ogere, Lokoja, or Jebba. WizIOT telematics units remain awake in low-power mode, sampling fuel levels every 30 seconds and sending immediate SMS alarms if fuel levels drop unexpectedly without ignition.

### Step 4: Automate Fuel Card & Receipt API Reconciliation
Match electronic fuel card purchases directly against physical in-tank sensor increases. If a driver submits a receipt for 300 liters but the capacitive probe records only 200 liters added to the tank, WizIOT software generates an instant **Fuel Short-Fill Mismatch Exception**.

### Step 5: Geofence Authorized Fueling Stations
Restrict filling station refueling to pre-approved, geofenced fuel depots (e.g., TotalEnergies, Mobil, Conoil stations). Refueling events occurring outside approved geofences trigger immediate non-compliance alerts to dispatchers.

### Step 6: Enclose Wiring Harnesses in Stainless Steel Armor
Drivers facing fuel monitoring often attempt to disable sensors by cutting wires. WizIOT installs flexible stainless steel armored conduits over all sensor wiring, paired with wire-cut tamper detection circuits that sound an audible cab horn alarm if wires are cut.

### Step 7: Tap CAN-Bus ECU for True Fuel Burn Metrics
Connect telematics hardware directly to the truck ECU (J1939 CAN-bus protocol) to log exact engine fuel consumption per kilometer. Cross-referencing engine fuel burn against trip distance exposes driver claims of "excessive traffic idling."

### Step 8: Multi-Tank Differential Volume Summation
Heavy haulage trucks transiting long routes frequently carry main and auxiliary tanks connected by equalizer hoses. WizIOT multi-tank algorithms sum total volume across both tanks simultaneously, preventing false alarms caused by fuel sloshing between tanks on uneven roads.

### Step 9: Establish Automated Driver Fuel Scorecards
Compile automated monthly Driver Fuel Scorecards rating drivers on actual CAN-bus fuel efficiency (km/liter), unverified fuel drop counts, and traffic idling time. 

### Step 10: Implement a Driver Fuel Savings Incentive Program
Combine technology with positive incentives. Drivers who maintain zero fuel variance and achieve top fuel efficiency scores receive a monthly cash bonus (e.g., ₦50,000 bonus), transforming drivers into partners in fuel conservation.

### Step 11: Mandate Tank Filler Neck Anti-Siphon Baffles
Fit heavy-duty internal steel mesh anti-siphon baffles inside truck filler necks to physically prevent hand hoses from reaching the bottom of fuel tanks, providing mechanical defense alongside electronic monitoring.

### Step 12: Conduct Monthly Audit Reviews with Management
Review automated monthly PDF fuel variance reports with executive leadership. Clear data graphs detailing siphoning locations, short-fill events, and driver rankings provide indisputable evidence for disciplinary actions or financial deductions.

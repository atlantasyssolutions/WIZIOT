In refrigerated transport logistics, auxiliary reefer engine fuel starvation is one of the most common causes of catastrophic cargo spoilage.

Refrigerated trailers feature independent diesel engines (Thermo King, Carrier) powered by dedicated 150L to 300L fuel tanks mounted under the trailer chassis. Drivers frequently check the truck's main fuel tank but forget to monitor the auxiliary reefer fuel level—leading to silent engine shutdown during long port waits or overnight stops.

If your fleet operates refrigerated trailers, relying on manual dipstick checks is a high-risk operational gamble. This guide explains how dedicated reefer fuel sensors and low-level SMS warnings prevent generator shutdowns.

## Why Auxiliary Reefer Fuel Monitoring is Critical

Auxiliary reefer engines operate under distinct fueling conditions compared to main truck engines:

1. **Independent Fuel Reservoirs:** Reefer units draw fuel from a separate underslung tank. Fuel levels are invisible to standard truck dashboard gauges.
2. **High Hourly Burn Rates:** Heavy reefer generators burning diesel in hot summer ambient temperatures consume 2.5 to 4.5 liters of fuel per hour, depleting a 200L tank within 48 to 60 hours of continuous operation.
3. **Reefer Tank Diesel Siphoning:** Thieves frequently siphon fuel from unmonitored reefer trailer tanks parked at rest stops, knowing drivers rarely check reefer fuel levels during mid-trip rest breaks.

WizIOT software enforces strict low-fuel alert thresholds to prevent generator starvation:
